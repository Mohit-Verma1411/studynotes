# Personality

==Personality== is the **dynamic, organized set of characteristics** that influences a person's thoughts, emotions, motivations and behaviour across situations. It is relatively stable but not rigidly fixed.

## Determinants of personality

| Determinant | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Heredity (biology)** | genes, physical build, temperament — the raw material |
| **Environment** | family, culture, schooling, peers, life experiences — shapes and modifies |
| **Situation** | the *current* context influences how personality is expressed |

> [!NOTE]
> **Heredity** sets the foundation (twin studies show strong heritability of traits); **environment** (culture, family, experience) develops it; **situation** decides which traits *show* right now — an extrovert can be quiet in a formal meeting.

## Personality traits influencing OB

The **Big Five (OCEAN)** model is the most accepted trait framework:

| Trait | High scorers tend to | OB relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Openness** | imaginative, curious, broad interests | creativity, adaptability |
| **Conscientiousness** | organized, dependable, disciplined | highest predictor of job performance |
| **Extraversion** | sociable, assertive, energetic | sales, leadership, teamwork |
| **Agreeableness** | cooperative, trusting, friendly | teamwork, service roles |
| **Neuroticism (emotional stability)** | anxious, moody (low = calm) | resilience, stress management |

Other relevant traits: **locus of control** (internal vs external), **self-esteem**, **self-monitoring**, **risk propensity**, **Type A/B** behaviour, and **Machiavellianism** (ethics implications).

> [!WARNING]
> **Personality–job fit matters.** Conscientiousness predicts performance in most jobs; extraversion suits social/sales roles; openness suits creative roles. A "great trait" in the wrong job still underperforms.

# Emotions

==Emotions== are **intense, short-lived feelings** directed at someone or something (anger at a colleague, joy at an award). They differ from **moods**, which are less intense and longer-lasting with no clear cause.

## Nature of emotions

- **Biological**: linked to physiological arousal (heart rate, hormones).
- **Universal**: basic emotions (happiness, sadness, anger, fear, disgust, surprise) are recognized across cultures.
- **Cognitive evaluation** triggers them: same event → different emotions for different people.
- **Visible**: expressed through facial expressions and body language.

## Emotional dimensions

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Valence** | pleasantness — positive (joy) vs negative (fear) |
| **Intensity / arousal** | strength of the feeling — calm → excited |
| **Frequency/duration** | how often and how long emotions occur |

**Emotional labour** — employees (e.g., hospitality, nursing) must display organizationally required emotions even when they feel otherwise; **emotional dissonance** results when felt and displayed emotions differ.

## Emotional Intelligence (EI)

==Emotional Intelligence== is the ability to **perceive, understand, use and manage** one's own and others' emotions.

Goleman's model (four/five competencies):

| Competency | Description |
|---|---|
| **Self-awareness** | knowing your own emotions in real time |
| **Self-management** | regulating emotions, staying calm, adapting |
| **Social awareness (empathy)** | sensing others' feelings and needs |
| **Relationship management** | influencing, inspiring, resolving conflict |

> [!TIP]
> EI predicts success in jobs with high interpersonal demands (managers, leaders, customer service) — often better than IQ alone. It can be developed with practice (feedback, mindfulness, empathy training).

# Perception and Individual Decision Making

==Perception== is the process by which individuals **organize and interpret** sensory information to give meaning to their environment. Two people see the *same* event differently.

## Factors influencing perception

| Factor group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Perceiver** | attitudes, motives, interests, past experience, expectations |
| **Target** | novelty, motion, sound, size, background, proximity, similarity |
| **Situation** | time, work setting, social context |

**Perceptual shortcuts (errors)**: stereotyping, halo effect (one good trait colours everything), contrast effect, projection, selective perception, first-impression bias.

> [!EXAMPLE]
|  | Two supervisors, same employee performance |
|---|---|
| **Supervisor A** (positive past experience with the employee) | sees diligent effort |
| **Supervisor B** (recently burned by an employee who `looked similar`) | sees careless work — selectivity/stereotyping at work |

# Rational Decision Making

The ==rational decision-making model== assumes the decision maker is perfectly logical:

| Step | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1. Define the problem | clear, complete statement of the issue |
| 2. Identify decision criteria | weights for what matters |
| 3. Allocate weights | relative importance of criteria |
| 4. Develop alternatives | all feasible options |
| 5. Evaluate alternatives | score each on the criteria |
| 6. Select the best | highest total score |

**Assumptions**: complete information, all alternatives known, clear preferences, no time/cost constraints, maximization of outcomes.

## Bounded Rationality

==Bounded rationality== (Simon) accepts that real decisions are made with **limited information, limited time, and limited cognitive capacity**. The decision maker:

- **Satisfices** — chooses the first acceptable option, not the optimal one.
- Uses **heuristics** (rules of thumb) and simplifies the problem.
- Operates within the limits of their "rationality".

| | Rational model | Bounded rationality |
|---|---|---|
| Information | complete | limited |
| Alternatives | all known | a few found |
| Criteria | weighted formally | simplified |
| Outcome | maximize | **satisfice** |
| Reality | ideal, rarely used | describes actual decisions |

# Leadership — Approaches

==Leadership== is the ability to **influence a group** toward the achievement of goals. It is a *process* (not a position) and involves followers, influence and goals.

## 1. Trait approaches

Early theories: leaders are **born** with distinguishing traits. Key traits found to matter:

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| **Drive** | ambition, energy, persistence |
| **Self-confidence** | belief in own ability |
| **Integrity** | honesty, trustworthiness |
| **Emotional intelligence** | self & social awareness |
| **Cognitive ability** | intelligence, analytical skill |
| **Extraversion** | sociability, assertiveness |

> [!WARNING]
> Traits alone **cannot predict** leadership — the research was inconclusive, so the field moved to *behaviours* and *situations*.

## 2. Behavioural approaches

Leaders are **made** — leadership is learned behaviour. Two classic dimensions: **task-oriented** (structure, planning, output) and **people-oriented** (support, relationship, trust).

| Study | Task orientation | People orientation |
|---|---|---|
| Ohio State | Initiating structure | Consideration |
| Michigan | Production-centred | Employee-centred |
| Blake–Mouton managerial grid | Concern for production | Concern for people |

The **Managerial Grid** plots both concerns 1–9: **Team management (9,9)** — high on both — was the ideal; (1,1) impoverished; (9,1) authority–compliance; (1,9) country-club.

## 3. Situational (contingency) approaches

"No one best style" — effective leadership depends on the **situation**.

| Model | Core idea |
|---|---|
| **Fiedler's LPC** | match leader orientation (task vs relationship) to situation favourableness (leader–member relations, task structure, position power) |
| **Hersey–Blanchard SLT** | adapt style (telling, selling, participating, delegating) to follower **readiness** |
| **Path–Goal** | leader clarifies paths and removes obstacles to follower goals; style suits the situation (directive, supportive, participative, achievement) |
| **House's style flexibility** | same leader can use different styles |

## 4. Emerging approaches

| Approach | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **Transformational** | inspires followers to exceed expectations, changes the culture |
| **Transactional** | exchanges rewards for performance (contingent reward, management by exception) |
| **Charismatic** | leader's vision, confidence and personal magnetism create devotion |
| **Servant** | leader puts followers' needs first |
| **Authentic** | leader is genuine, transparent, values-driven |
| **Ethical / shared / digital** | values-centred, distributed teams, tech-enabled influence |

> [!EXAMPLE]
> **Transformational vs Transactional**: a *transactional* leader says "meet your target, get your bonus"; a *transformational* leader creates a vision ("we will be #1") that makes people work beyond self-interest. Both are useful — transactional maintains, transformational changes.
