# Introduction to Organizational Behaviour

==Organizational Behaviour (OB)== is the systematic study of **what people do in organizations** and how their **behaviour affects organization performance**. It draws on psychology, sociology, social psychology, anthropology and political science.

> [!DEFINITION]
> OB studies the actions and attitudes of people in organizations: individuals, groups, and the structure that holds them together, with the goal of applying that knowledge to make organizations work more effectively.

## Importance of OB

| Reason | What it gives |
|---|---|
| **Understanding & predicting** | anticipate what people will do in given situations |
| **Managing people effectively** | motivate, coordinate, resolve conflict |
| **Improving productivity** | aligned goals, engaged workforce |
| **Better decision making** | uses evidence, not intuition alone |
| **Human resource perspective** | people are the most valuable asset |
| **Change management** | helps organizations adapt to change |
| **Work-life balance & well-being** | healthier, more committed employees |

# Role of Managers in OB

**Managers** get things done **through other people**. Their four functions:

| Function | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Planning** | decide goals and how to achieve them |
| **Organizing** | arrange tasks, people and resources |
| **Leading** | direct, motivate, communicate |
| **Controlling** | monitor progress and correct deviations |

**Mintzberg's managerial roles** (three groups):

- **Interpersonal** — figurehead, leader, liaison.
- **Informational** — monitor, disseminator, spokesperson.
- **Decisional** — entrepreneur, disturbance handler, resource allocator, negotiator.

Managers also need **technical** skills (job-specific), **human** skills (work with people) and **conceptual** skills (see the big picture). As managers rise, human and conceptual skills matter more.

# Foundation of Individual Behaviour

## Biographical characteristics

Easily observable personal factors that influence behaviour — **age, gender, marital status, tenure, education, religious/ethnic background**.

| Characteristic | Typical OB observations |
|---|---|
| **Age** | older workers: less turnover/absenteeism, lower accident rates; innovation often younger |
| **Gender** | no consistent difference in job performance; issues are cultural |
| **Marital status** | married employees often lower turnover and absenteeism |
| **Tenure** | longer tenure → lower turnover, higher satisfaction |
| **Education** | influences training readiness and job assignment |
| **Religion / ethnicity** | shape values and attitudes; must be respected (diversity) |

> [!TIP]
> Biographical characteristics are the *demographics* of behaviour — useful for prediction, but OB is confident only when they are combined with deeper factors like ability and personality.

## Abilities

==Ability== is the capacity to perform tasks — the physical and mental capabilities a person brings to a job.

### Types of ability

| Type | Components |
|---|---|
| **Intellectual abilities** | number aptitude, verbal comprehension, perceptual speed, inductive/deductive reasoning, spatial visualization, memory |
| **Physical abilities** | strength (dynamic, trunk, static, explosive), flexibility, other factors (coordination, balance, stamina) |

- Ability–job **fit** matters: match skills to demands, else performance and satisfaction drop.
- Intellectual ability is central to most professional/technical jobs (measured by IQ-style tests); physical ability for athletes, technicians, etc.

## Values

==Values== are *basic convictions* about what is personally/ socially preferable — they influence attitudes, motivation and decisions.

| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| **Content** | *what* is important (freedom, security, achievement) |
| **Intensity** | *how important* it is |

**Rokeach value survey**:
- **Terminal values** — desirable end-states (prosperity, peace, happiness, freedom).
- **Instrumental values** — preferred *means* to ends (honesty, ambition, courage, helpfulness).

> [!NOTE]
> Values are relatively **stable and long-lasting**, formed early through family, culture and schooling. Attitudes are more changeable — hence the effort to improve attitudes at work.

## Attitudes

==Attitude== is a *evaluative statement* — "favourable or unfavourable feeling" toward a person, object or event.

### Three components of attitude

| Component | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive** | belief/thought | "My boss is fair" |
| **Affective** | feeling/emotion | "I like my workplace" |
| **Behavioural** | intention to act | "I will stay and work harder" |

### Types / key attitudes in OB

| Job attitude | About |
|---|---|
| **Job satisfaction** | positive feeling about one's job |
| **Job involvement** | psychological identification with the job |
| **Organizational commitment** | loyalty to, and identification with, the organization |
| **Perceived organizational support (POS)** | belief that the organization values you |

**Cognitive dissonance** — when attitudes and behaviour conflict (smoking despite knowing the risks), people feel tension and try to reduce it (change attitude, behaviour, or rationalize).

## Attitude and Workforce Diversity

==Diversity== refers to differences among people: age, gender, race, ethnicity, disability, religion, education, culture, personality.

| Benefit of diversity | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Broader ideas and creativity | inclusive culture |
| Better problem solving | psychological safety to speak up |
| Better market understanding | mirror diverse customers |
| Higher talent pool | unbiased hiring |

**Attitudes toward diversity** matter: positive, respectful attitudes convert diversity into performance; prejudice and stereotypes destroy it. Managers use **diversity training**, fair policies and inclusive leadership to shape attitudes.

> [!EXAMPLE]
> A team with members from different cultures: attitudes of openness (cognitive: "different views help"; affective: interest; behavioural: inviting input) turn variety into better decisions. Stereotyped attitudes produce conflict and groupthink.
